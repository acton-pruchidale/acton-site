    What the cold means for a trailer

    Steps light up for the coldest temperature in the forecast.

    1. 0 °C and colder

      Anything outside the heated underbelly can freeze

      A plain hose, the spigot, and the spot where the hose meets the cold ground go first. Heat tape goes directly on the pipe (never over insulation) and gets checked every fall for cracks or charring.

    2. −15 °C and colder

      The water hose is the weak point

      My supervisor, who lives in his fifth wheel all year in Alberta, used a heated hose with heat tape, insulation and a covered spigot box while it was merely cold. In deep cold he takes the hose off and runs from the trailer's own fresh tank, filled from a water bladder. He also never lets the holding tanks fill right up.

    3. −18 °C and colder

      Propane pressure starts to drop

      Propane only turns to gas because of the pressure in the tank, and that pressure falls as the tank gets colder. Published figures for the tank's pressure:

      about −18 °C24.5 psi about −23 °C17.5 psi about −29 °C11.5 psi about −34 °C6.8 psi about −42 °Cno gas at all

      Drawing gas chills the tank further, and a low tank gives off gas more slowly, so go into a cold snap with a fuller tank. A 100 lb tank lasts my supervisor one to two weeks at about −20 °C. Never heat a propane tank to raise the pressure.

    Never, at any temperature

    • An open flame or torch on lines or valves
    • Boiling water down the drains
    • Running the water pump on frozen lines

    If it does freeze: get the heat back and let the trailer warm up slowly. When his froze, it took the furnaces between 12 and 24 hours to bring the water back.

    This is general information from one person's experience and published sources, not a safety inspection. Keep working CO and propane alarms, and check your own setup.
